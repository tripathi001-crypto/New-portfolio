The problem: Generic mock interview tools gave surface-level feedback — no PM-specific depth, no structure coaching, no sense of what a real hiring panel would think.

What I built: I owned this end to end, solo — AI workflows, UX, architecture, LLM integrations, payments, and monetization, from concept to a live paid product.

The Problem
Every mock interview tool felt like a chatbot, not a coach
Job seekers were prepping with tools that gave surface-level feedback — "good answer!" — with zero PM-specific depth or framework coaching.
The Insight
ATS optimization gets you the interview. Confidence gets you the offer.
Most tools solve resume or interview prep, not both. The real drop-off happens in the gap between getting shortlisted and freezing in the room.
The Decision
Build one platform, with AI that probes like a PM interviewer
Scoped v1 to two jobs: scan resumes against real JDs for ATS gaps, and run structured mock interviews that push on STAR structure, product sense, and metrics — not generic praise.
The Outcome
300+ users, a live paid tier, grown through word of mouth
No paid marketing. Adoption spread through PM communities. The insight held — users didn't just want a tool, they wanted to feel prepared.
Technical depth

Built with React on the frontend and OpenAI's API for resume analysis, skill-gap detection, and interview simulation. The workflow chains multiple prompts — one to parse and score the resume against a target role, another to generate role-specific mock interview questions, and a third to score responses and produce structured, actionable feedback rather than generic praise.

Payments and the ₹199 Pro plan run through a self-integrated checkout flow. I designed the full UX myself, then handled deployment, monitoring, and iteration based on real user behavior — without a team.

MakeMyTrip · B2C Travel

Enabling Seamless Group Travel

How do you get 6 friends to agree on a flight — and actually book it together?

🔍
The Problem
Group travel is broken by design

Individual booking flows assume one decision-maker. Group trips involve multiple people, budgets, and preferences — users were bouncing to spreadsheets and screenshots to coordinate.

💡
The Insight
The coordinator is the real user — not the group

Research showed one person always organizes. The product needed to serve them first — proposing options, collecting votes, locking in decisions — without the group leaving the app.

⚙️
The Solution
A collaborative booking layer on top of existing flows

Designed shareable trip proposals, in-app preference voting, budget pooling, and a group lock-in mechanism — without disrupting the existing solo booking experience.

📈
The Outcome
One coordination flow, replacing scattered external tools

Group organisers could propose, vote, and lock in a trip inside a single flow — replacing the spreadsheets and screenshot threads used before.

Shaadi.com · B2C Matchmaking

Building Trust Through Verification

In a space where trust is everything — how do you design a badge system that feels safe, not intrusive?

🔍
The Problem
Fake profiles were hurting trust and retention

Fake or misrepresented profiles weren't just a UX issue — they were a safety and business risk. Users were disengaging after matches felt untrustworthy.

💡
The Insight
Trust is felt before it's read — it's visual, not textual

Users scan for visual signals, not disclaimers. The insight was a tiered badge system communicating credibility at a glance, with progressive disclosure for more detail.

⚙️
The Solution
A 3-tier trust badge framework

Designed three levels — ID Verified, Photo Verified, Premium Verified — each with distinct visual treatment. Higher trust required more steps, framed as a privilege, not a burden.

📈
The Outcome
A clearer, layered trust signal across profiles

The badge system gave users a visual way to gauge credibility at a glance, paired with a reporting flow tied to badge revocation.
